Utah County Payroll

Where did your tax money go? Payroll.

Between 2019 and 2024 Utah County raised its property tax twice and rolled back only part of it in between. Here is one place the money went, and who decided it.

The Commissioners' Pay

While your taxes rose, so did their salaries.

While county property taxes rose, the county commissioners' own salaries climbed from about $119,000 a year to nearly $170,000.

The Tax Math

In five years, the county's tax nearly doubled.

Take the county's own tax changes from 2019 to 2024 and stack them, one on top of the other. Start with $100 of county property tax before 2019:

Before 2019
$100

Flat for two decades, as her site puts it.

2019
$167

Raised 67.4% after a Truth in Taxation hearing.

2021
$134

About half of the 2019 increase repealed: 25% of it in April, 49.7% in all by June.

2024
$198

Raised another 47.99%, adopted in full in December.

≈ 98% higher

$100 × (1 + 0.674 × 0.503) × 1.4799 ≈ $198

That total is arithmetic on the published percentages for the county's portion of the property tax, and nothing more. It is not a line in a county ledger. The 2019 and 2024 increases and the April 2021 repeal are as documented on Gina Tanner's own campaign site, auditorgina.com. The June 2021 total is from the Salt Lake Tribune. KSL reported the 2024 increase adopted in full on December 18, 2024.

"This isn't about the county's employees. Most anyone would take a raise if it were offered. It's about the officials who approved spending beyond what the county took in, and never made that plain to the citizens paying for it."

— Hans Andersen
