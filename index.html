Why Hans

An auditor should answer to you — not to city hall.

The County Auditor is the taxpayer's accountant. It's the one office whose whole job is to make sure your money is counted honestly, spent lawfully, and reported openly. The $50 Million Win

The tax hike Orem stopped — and never paid.

In August 2012 the Mayor and five councilmen moved to raise Orem property taxes by $3,345,000 a year. Hans led the fight against it — half of it was beaten in the council chamber, the other half at the ballot box. None of it was ever collected.

$50,175,000

Orem families have kept an estimated $50,175,000 since the 2012 council vote.

2012 2026

$3,345,000 a year × 15 years — money the city never took.

That total is the arithmetic above and nothing more: the full annual increase, none of it ever levied, counted from the 2012 council vote through 2026. It is not a line in a city ledger.

Half stopped in the room

The public hearing opened at 6 p.m. on Tuesday, August 14, 2012 and ran past 2 a.m. — eight hours of Orem residents saying no, hundreds of them. Before the night was over, the mayor cut the proposal in half, to $1.7 million, on the spot.

Half stopped at the ballot box

Citizens forced what was left onto the ballot. On November 5, 2013, 71.26% of Orem voters rejected the increase — 69.16% in the first count, climbing as the remaining ballots were tallied.

"Who made better use of that $50 million — Orem City, or Orem families?"

— Hans Andersen
