## About H. Verlan Andersen (for the site & bot)

Hans's father: attorney, BYU professor of accounting, and later an LDS General
Authority (Seventy). His books argue a natural-rights political philosophy —
government is organized force; that force is only justified to protect life,
liberty, and property; and every citizen is personally, morally accountable for
what government does with their vote. Hans helped compile his father's writings
into "The Works of H. Verlan Andersen." This heritage is the intellectual root
of Hans's "proper role of government" convictions.

## Book 1: Many Are Called But Few Are Chosen (1967)

### Overview
A short book arguing that how a citizen votes to use government force is a moral
act for which he is personally accountable. Government is nothing but organized
force; that force is legitimate only for protecting life, liberty, and property;
using it for redistribution, subsidies, and regulation of honest work is
"unrighteous dominion." Framing is explicitly LDS (addressed to priesthood
holders, grounded in D&C and the Book of Mormon), but the political core is a
classic limited-government natural-law argument that stands on its own.

### Core principles
- Government is force. Every law is ultimately backed by threats against life,
  liberty, or property; there is no neutral or costless government program.
- Citizens are personally responsible for what government does in their name —
  voting for a law makes you complicit in the coercion it authorizes.
- The Golden Rule is the test of legislation: force through government is
  justified only if you'd concede it just when applied to you.
- Legitimate functions are narrow: punishing crime, defensive war, enforcing
  property rights and contracts, and taxing only enough to pay for those.
- Property is as essential to freedom as life and liberty; control of a person's
  property or subsistence is control of his will.
- Redistribution is legalized taking: government can give nothing without first
  taking it from someone else.
- Power corrupts predictably — officeholders enlarge their functions unless
  watched by a moral, alert, informed electorate.
- Dependency corrupts the electorate: subsidies seduce citizens into supporting
  ever-larger government.
- Compelled charity is not charity — force destroys the voluntary moral choice.
- Licensing/occupational gatekeeping is overreach.

## Book 2: The Great and Abominable Church of the Devil (1972)

### Overview
The more doctrinal, apocalyptic companion volume: argues from LDS scripture that
the prophesied "church of the devil" is corrupted government — organized force
used to destroy free agency. Heavily theological throughout (Satan, prophecy,
scripture on nearly every page); underlying it is the same natural-rights
argument. Chapter IV ("the elements of freedom": life, liberty, property,
knowledge) contains the most campaign-usable material.

### Core principles
- Moral law applies to group action: people commit acts through government they
  would condemn in themselves.
- Delegated power can rise no higher than its source — citizens cannot delegate
  a right they don't individually possess.
- Property is life in stored form: taking property takes the part of a person's
  life spent acquiring it. (Root of his tax skepticism.)
- One moral test for every law: does its purpose preserve or destroy the
  elements of freedom?
- Graduated taxation is unjust; proportionality is fair. (summary-sourced)
- Chapters on socialism-as-slavery and public education as "priestcraft" are
  religiously framed and politically volatile — legacy pages only, if at all.

### Verbatim quotes (verified against the book PDF, Preface–Ch. VI)
1. "A man's property is his life. It is what he spends his productive life to obtain and it is that with which he sustains life." — Ch. IV  ← strongest campaign line
2. "When you take from a person his property, you take from him his life—that part of his life he spent acquiring it." — Ch. IV
3. "Property is the fruit of labor and naturally belongs to that person whose efforts created it." — Ch. IV (closely echoes a famous Lincoln line — VERIFY attribution before crediting Andersen)
4. "It is the duty of government to protect the elements of freedom by punishing those who intentionally and without justification commit crimes against them." — Ch. IV
5. "An act is good or evil depending upon whether its purpose is to preserve or destroy the elements of freedom." — Ch. IV
6. "The fostering of full economic freedom lies at the base of our liberties." — Ch. IV (likely quoting DAVID O. McKAY — verify)
